## PromptTAD: Object-Prompt Enhanced Traffic Anomaly Detection 

Hao Qiu , Xiaobo Yang , and Xiaojin Gong 

**_Abstract_ —Ego-centric Traffic Anomaly Detection (TAD) aims to identify abnormal events in videos captured by dashboardmounted cameras in vehicles. Compared to anomaly detection in roadside surveillance videos, ego-centric TAD poses greater challenges due to the dynamic backgrounds caused by vehicle motion. Previous frame-level methods are often vulnerable to interference from these dynamic backgrounds and struggle to detect small objects located at a distance or off-center. To address these challenges, we propose an object-prompt enhanced method that integrates detected traffic objects into a frame-level TAD framework. Our approach introduces an object-prompt scheme comprising an object prompt encoder, along with two cross-attention-based aggregation modules: an instance-wise aggregation module for fusing information between object instances and the scene, and a relation-wise aggregation module for capturing relationships interobjects. Additionally, we design an instance-level loss to supervise anomaly detection at the object level. Our method effectively mitigates interference from dynamic backgrounds, improves the detection of distant or off-center anomalies, and enables precise spatial localization of anomalies. Experimental results on the DoTA and DADA-2000 datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance.** 

**_Index Terms_ —Deep learning for visual perception, object detection, segmentation and categorization.**

Over the past few years, various approaches have been developed for ego-centric TAD, employing either frame-level [5], [6] or object-level [1], [7] techniques. Frame-level methods can be implemented in an end-to-end manner, thus avoiding error propagation from pre-processing steps. However, the framelevel representations are unable to spatially localize anomalous objects and fail to mitigate the interference from dynamic backgrounds. Moreover, the global perspective often emphasizes prominent regions within the frames, resulting in the neglect of distant or off-center objects, as shown in Fig. 1. In contrast, object-centric methods focus on detected objects, enabling more targeted anomaly detection while significantly reducing the impact of dynamic backgrounds. Thanks to recent advancements in object detection techniques, objects—whether small, near the edge of the field of view, or present in cluttered backgrounds—can now be reliably identified in most cases. This accurate detection provides a solid foundation for subsequent anomaly detection. 

In this work, we aim to integrate frame-level and object-aware techniques to enhance traffic anomaly detection (TAD). Unlike previous object-centric approaches that detect anomalies by tracking and predicting object locations [1], [8] or by modeling

Prompt-enhanced techniques [11], [12] have recently achieved substantial success in image segmentation and other vision tasks, demonstrating that introducing position-awarepromptscaneffectivelydirectamodel’sattention to specific areas of interest. Specifically, we build our method on the MOVAD baseline [5], which utilizes a Video Swin Transformer [13] to extract frame-level features from each video clip and an LSTM [14] to capture long-term dependencies across clips. To enhance this framework, we design an object-prompt scheme that integrates traffic objects detected by YOLOv9 [15] into the frame-level baseline. The designed prompt scheme includes an object-prompt encoder, together with two cross-attention based components: an instance-wise aggregation module, which fuses information from each object instance with the overall scene context, and a relation-wise aggregation module, which captures interactions and relationships among objects. Furthermore, in addition to the standard frame-level loss, we introduce an objectinstance loss to specifically supervise the anomaly detection of individual objects. 

traffic anomaly detection in dashcam videos has attracted increasing research interest in recent years. Based on the type of supervision, existing TAD methods can be roughly categorized into unsupervised/self-supervised and supervised techniques. Unsupervised [1], [16] or self-supervised [10], [17] methods rely on normal videos for anomaly detection, typically employing reconstruction- or prediction-based techniques. Due to the lack of abnormal samples in training, their performance is often limited. Recently,withtheavailabilityoflarge-scaledatasets [1], [2] that contain temporally and even spatially annotated traffic videos, various supervised methods [5], [7] leveraging classification or anomaly regression techniques have been developed, achieving superior performance in detecting anomalies. 

On the other hand, these TAD methods [18], [19] can also be categorized into frame-level and object-centric approaches. For instance, the frame-level method MOVAD [5] captures spatiotemporal correlations of current and recent frames using a Video Swin Transformer and gathers long-term contextual information through an LSTM for anomaly classification. TTHF [6] adopts the CLIP [20] framework to align video clips with text prompts while enhancing frame-level visual representation by integrating high-frequency information. In contrast, the objectcentric method FOL [1] detects anomalies by tracking and predicting object positions. STFE [7] captures and encodes the spatial relationships of detected objects for the fine identification of potential accident frames. Distinct from these methods, our work combines a frame-level baseline with object prompts, achieving a more effective solution for TAD. 

The contributions of this work can be summarized as follows: 

- r We introduce an object-prompt scheme into a frame-level baseline, utilizing detected foreground objects to guide the model’s attention toward these regions. This approach not only strengthens the focus on foreground elements but also captures the relationships between objects and the scene, as well as interactions among objects, effectively reducing interference from dynamic backgrounds and enhancing the detection of distant and off-center anomalies. 

- r We utilize bipartite matching, based on the Hungarian algorithm, to classify detected foreground objects as either anomalous or non-anomalous. Based on the pseudo labels obtained from the classification results, we design an object-instance loss to supervise anomaly detection at the individual object level, enabling precise spatial localization of anomalies. 

- r Experimental results on the DoTA dataset [1] show that ourproposedmethodachievesstate-of-the-artperformance in both the AUC and STAUC metrics. Additionally, it attains state-of-the-art results on the DADA-2000 dataset [2] without any fine-tuning, excelling particularly in detecting non-ego-involved anomalies that often occur outside the central field of view.

### _B. Prompt-Enhanced Learning_ 

Recently, prompt-based techniques have gained widespread adoption in both natural language processing and computer vision. Prompts provide additional context or specific instructions that help models to focus on relevant features or tasks, thereby enhancing performance. For instance, various manually designed or learnable textual and visual prompts [21], [22] have been developed to adapt CLIP [20] and other large pre-trained foundation models for a range of downstream tasks, including grasping detection [23], global localization [24], and semantic segmentation [25], among others. The vision foundation model SAM [11] also explores diverse prompt types, such as points, boxes, and masks, to guide image segmentation. 

In video anomaly detection (VAD), a task closely related to traffic anomaly detection in surveillance videos, various promptenhanced learning techniques have also been developed. For example, Chen et al. [26] integrated learnable abnormal or normal prompts with text prompts to inject semantic priors into visual features, improving VAD performance. Pu et al. [27] proposed knowledge-based prompts that retrieve relevant semantics from ConceptNet to enhance the discrimination of visual features. Similarly, Tao et al. [28] employed a textual prompt dictionary of anomaly events for VAD. In contrast to these learnable or textual prompts, our work, inspired by SAM [11], utilizes bounding boxes obtained from object detection as prompts to guide the model’s attention to foreground objects. This approach mitigates the issue of over-focusing on the center of the field of view while enabling spatial localization of anomalous objects.

### III. THE PROPOSED METHOD 

Fig. 2 presents an overview of the proposed method. Our framework is built upon the MOVAD baseline [5], which employs a Video Swin Transformer (VST) [13] to extract framelevel features from each short-term video clip and an LSTM [14] to capture long-term dependencies across clips. To guide the model’s attention to foreground objects while preserving scene context, we introduce a prompt-based scheme that integrates object detection results into the baseline. Specifically, we design an instance-wise aggregation module to fuse information between each object instance and the scene, and a relation-wise aggregation module to fuse information among objects. In our approach, the object prompts help the frame-level features to focus more effectively on foreground objects, improving the detection of small or off-center anomalies and reducing the interference of dynamic backgrounds. Meanwhile, the framelevel features provide contextual information to the object-level features, enabling accurate spatial anomaly localization.

### _A. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics_ 

We evaluate the proposed method using two datasets: DoTA [1] and DADA-2000 [2]. 

_DoTA_ [1] is an ego-centric traffic anomaly dataset collected using dash cameras across various locations, weather conditions, and lighting scenarios. It contains 4,677 videos with a resolution of 1280 _×_ 720 pixels, accompanied by temporal, spatial, and categorical annotations. We follow the standard train-test split used in previous works [5], [6], [7] to evaluate our method. To further assess performance in scenarios involving small objects, we construct a specialized subset by selecting scenes where 

abnormal targets have bounding boxes smaller than 2,267pixels, which corresponds to the size of the 10% smallest targets in the dataset. By this means, we filter out 334 videos from the original 1,402 test videos to form the subset. 

_DADA_ -2000 [2] is a dataset designed for driver attention prediction in driving accident scenarios. It provides temporal annotations for accident intervals and spatial annotations based on drivers’ eye-gaze. The dataset consists of 2,000 video sequences, each with a resolution of 1584 _×_ 660 pixels, categorized into 54 anomaly types, which are further grouped into two major sets: ego-carinvolvedandego-caruninvolved.wefollowthetrain-test split used in [2], [6], but evaluate our model exclusively on the test set without training on the training set. 

_Evaluation Metrics:_ We use the Area Under the Curve (AUC) metric to evaluate the performance of frame-level anomaly detection. To incorporate spatial information into the evaluation, we also include the Spatial-Temporal Area Under the Curve (STAUC) [1] score as an additional metric. In contrast to AUC that focuses solely on per-frame anomaly scores, STAUC takes into account the spatial overlap between predicted anomalous regions and annotated ground truth, providing a more comprehensive evaluation by considering both spatial and temporal aspects of anomaly detection. 

### _B. Implementation Details_ 

We implement our model in PyTorch and train it on four NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. The training setup employs the SGD optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.002, momentum of 0.9, weight decay of 0.00001, and a batch size of 16. The model is trained for a total of 200 epochs. Following [5], the input video resolution is set to 640 _×_ 480 with each video clip containing _NF_ = 4 frames. The video clip length, defined as the number of each video frames in the training batch, is set to 8. Additionally, theweightingfactor is _α_ = 0 _._ 7, andtheVSTis initializedusinga model pretrained on the Something-Something V2 dataset [33]. The feature channel dimension at the lowest scale of the VST, which is also the token size, is _DE_ = 256. The model includes _LI_ = 4 blocks in the instance-aggregation module, and _LR_ = 4 blocks in the relation-aggregation module. Additionally, the number of learnable query tokens is set to _NK_ = 12.

### V. CONCLUSION
